    01 / Business Context

    Evaluate whether a new Pickup channel delivers business value

    Pickup was introduced to expand customer convenience and create another purchase option. The business challenge was to assess channel performance beyond sales volume: understand how Pickup performed against its contribution target, monitor comparable-store performance and identify its relationship with existing sales channels.

    Business questionHow can management distinguish channel performance, store-level sales movement and the actual commercial value of the Pickup initiative?
    02 / Data Story

    Channel performance needs both a sales view and a like-for-like store view

    Channel contribution

    Track Pickup sales against the channel target and monitor its share of total sales.

    Comparable-store performance

    Compare ADS across matched stores to understand the underlying sales trend without mixing in store-count changes.

    Existing-channel movement

    Review Dine-In, Take Away and Online movement alongside Pickup to understand changes in channel mix.

    03 / Methodology & Calculation Logic

    Build a comparable baseline and evaluate sales movement per store

    The GAR analysis uses a 28-day pre-launch baseline from 21 July to 17 August 2026. The post-launch period begins on 18 August 2026. ADS per store is used to normalize store performance across the baseline and GAR periods.

    01
    Establish the baseline

    Calculate average daily sales per store for the 28-day pre-launch period.

    02
    Prepare the GAR-period view

    Calculate average daily sales per store from the launch date through the review period.

    03
    Map channel movement

    Aggregate Dine-In, Take Away, Online and Pickup at store and date level.

    04
    Normalize store performance

    Compare ADS per store, rather than total sales, to reduce distortion from different store counts.

    05
    Classify store-level movement

    Apply the GAR cannibalization and impact-status rules to identify different store patterns.

    06
    Monitor the like-for-like cohort

    Compare 2025 and 2026 ADS across matched stores and review daily performance against a same-weekday baseline.

    Case Study Details
    01 / Business Context

    Manage store performance with more than a sales ranking

    Stores can achieve different outcomes across sales and profitability. A store may reach its sales benchmark but miss the GP3 objective, while another may protect GP3 but still have room to grow sales. Management needs a clear way to distinguish these situations and assign different priorities instead of applying one action plan to every store.

    02 / Data Story

    Four performance segments emerge from the Sales × GP3 quadrant

    Each store is positioned using sales achievement against its benchmark and GP3 margin. The quadrant turns two business measures into four actionable store profiles.

    STARSSales ≥ 100% · GP3 ≥ 15%

    Strong sales and healthy profitability. Protect performance and replicate good practices.

    BLOOMERSSales < 100% · GP3 ≥ 15%

    Healthy margin with sales upside. Focus on unlocking demand and transactions.

    MATURESSales ≥ 100% · GP3 < 15%

    Sales are achieved, but profitability needs recovery through cost and mix improvement.

    LAGGARDSSales < 100% · GP3 < 15%

    Both dimensions need attention. Prioritize root-cause diagnosis and a focused recovery plan.

    Track movement, not just bucket position

    Month-to-month transition analysis follows each store from its previous bucket to its latest bucket. The movement matrix distinguishes stores that improve, remain stable or decline, and shows where management attention may be needed.

    03 / Methodology

    From store-level metrics to differentiated action

    Sales AchievementActual Sales ÷ Store Sales Benchmark × 100%Compares store sales with its selected sales benchmark.
    GP3 MarginGP3 ÷ Net Sales × 100%Measures profitability relative to net sales.
    01Prepare the store dataset

    Align sales, GP3, period and store master attributes such as Code GIS, RM, AM, city and province.

    02Calculate both KPIs

    Calculate sales achievement and GP3 margin at one row per store for each review period.

    03Assign quadrant

    Apply the sales and GP3 thresholds consistently to assign Stars, Bloomers, Matures or Laggards.

    04Analyze portfolio patterns

    Compare bucket distribution by region, RM, AM, city and store maturity.

    05Measure MTM movement

    Match stores across consecutive periods and classify their transitions between buckets.

    06Translate insight into action

    Set differentiated priorities for sales growth, GP3 recovery, performance protection and store turnaround.

    Classification logic: Sales achievement is split at 100% and GP3 margin at 15%.

    Action priorities by segment

    STARS · Sustain & Replicate
    • Protect service, product quality and store execution.
    • Identify practices that can be replicated by other stores.
    • Maintain sales growth without diluting GP3.
    BLOOMERS · Unlock Sales Potential
    • Review traffic, transactions and average check opportunities.
    • Improve local activation and product availability.
    • Grow sales while protecting healthy GP3.
    MATURES · Recover Profitability
    • Review food cost, discounts, waste and product mix.
    • Assess manpower productivity and controllable costs.
    • Set GP3 recovery actions while maintaining sales.
    LAGGARDS · Focused Recovery
    • Diagnose root causes across sales drivers and cost structure.
    • Set store-specific sales and GP3 recovery priorities.
    • Apply closer follow-up with clear owners and review cadence.

    Case Study Details
    01 / Business Context

    Turn historical sales into a preparation plan the store can execute

    Store teams need a clear answer to four operational questions: what component to prepare, how much to make, when it should be ready and how long it can be held. Without a demand-based preparation plan, production quantities and timing can become disconnected from actual menu demand, recipe usage, batch sizes and holding-time limits.

    02 / Data Story

    Build the preparation baseline from September 2026 demand

    BASELINE PERIODSeptember 2026
    STORE COVERAGE188 stores
    DEMAND GRAINHourly · Store · Menu
    DAY TYPEWeekday · Friday · Weekend

    Historical sales were analyzed by store, day type and hour to establish the demand pattern for each menu item. The menu-level demand was then converted into preparation-component requirements using recipe usage and preparation master data. This creates a more practical basis for deciding preparation quantity and timing than using one fixed quantity across every day type.

    03 / Methodology

    Clean the source data, model component demand and schedule each batch

    Transaction cleaning

    Filter the September baseline, retain valid completed transactions, remove invalid quantities and handle duplicate transaction-detail records.

    Text and key standardization

    Trim and standardize menu names, component labels and store keys; map source item names to the canonical menu master.

    Master-data validation

    Validate menu-to-recipe mapping, component usage per sale, batch size, batch multiple, preparation duration and holding time.

    Demand aggregation

    Aggregate sales by store, date, day type, hour and menu item before converting menu demand into component demand.

    BigQuery · SQL exampleSeptember 2026 baseline
    View full SQL ↗
    -- Clean and standardize historical sales
SELECT
  DATE(order_datetime) AS sales_date,
  EXTRACT(HOUR FROM order_datetime) AS sales_hour,
  TRIM(store_code) AS store_code,
  UPPER(TRIM(menu_item)) AS menu_item,
  SAFE_CAST(quantity AS NUMERIC) AS qty_sold
FROM `YOUR_PROJECT.YOUR_DATASET.sales_transaction_items`
WHERE DATE(order_datetime)
  BETWEEN DATE '2026-09-01' AND DATE '2026-09-30'
  AND SAFE_CAST(quantity AS NUMERIC) > 0
  AND UPPER(TRIM(order_status))
      NOT IN ('VOID', 'CANCELLED', 'REFUNDED');
    Average hourly demandSUM(Historical Menu Qty) ÷ Observed Days by Store & Day TypeEstablishes a comparable demand baseline for Weekday, Friday and Weekend.
    Component demandAverage Menu Demand × Recipe Usage per SaleConverts expected menu sales into required preparation component quantity.
    Batch roundingCEILING(Component Demand ÷ 5) × 5Rounds preparation quantity to the configured batch multiple of 5.
    Estimated batch countCEILING(Planned Prep Qty ÷ Batch Size)Calculates the number of batches based on component batch size.
    Ready-to-use timePreparation Start + Prep DurationSets the time when a prepared component becomes available for use.
    Maximum use timeReady-to-use Time + Holding TimeDefines the end of the component's usable holding window.
    Google Sheets formula examples=CEILING(Demand_Qty/Batch_Multiple,1)*Batch_Multiple=ROUNDUP(Planned_Prep_Qty/Batch_Size,0)=Prep_Start+(Prep_Duration_Min/1440)=Ready_Time+(Holding_Hours/24)

    Operational scheduling logic

    H-1Previous day

    Preparation scheduled before the next operating day.

    HCurrent day

    Preparation aligned to the current operating hour.

    H+1Following day

    Overnight schedule for the next day's requirement.

    LAST BATCHBefore closing

    Final preparation before closing, considering holding time.
